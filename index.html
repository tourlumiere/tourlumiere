On this prototype

Schöffer gives the Tour Lumière Cybernétique two programmes, and they do not quite coincide. The Programme du calculateur (Appendix 5, pp. 108–111) states the general case: Boolean functions Xi of the normalised inputs, organs dealt by lot into groups, and, for each function that fires, a random duration Yi and a randomly drawn mode of operation. The Simulation du fonctionnement (Appendix 6, pp. 112–124), run in Fortran IV on a CDC 6400, reduces the tower to six windows and thirty-three outputs, and supplies what the general programme lacks, namely the coefficient of excitation D computed over ten binary sensors and the three régimes it selects. The prototype runs both together. The régime of the simulation governs colour, intensity and the validating draw; the general programme decides which organs are commanded, for how long and in what order, across the full inventory of the 1973 text (2,250 projectors, 2,085 flashes, 40 marine projectors, 24 lasers, 263 mirrors on 114 axes).

The form of the tower

The structure is traced from the sheet Façade et circulation verticale (p. 132). Read against Annex 7, the drawing yields a scale of about 0.23 m to the unit: its five platforms fall at 91, 142, 231, 267 and 300 m, where the structural calculation places them at 92, 144, 236, 272 and 307 m. The core measures some 36 m on façade 2 and 23 m on section 6; the cantilevered arms come in pairs, and it is between these pairs that the first vertical axes are set, which is how the night renderings (pp. 87, 89) show the mirrors. A wider frame encloses the lower 76 m, and the socle is an inverted trapezoid, 70 m at the slab. The sheet shows only the arms lying in its two planes; the rest of the two hundred named on p. 26 are placed within the envelope those arms define. The maquette photographed on p. 86 belongs to an earlier, denser state of the project and has not been followed. The façade can be laid over the rendering from the Documents tab.

The site

The first maquette (1963) placed the tower directly in front of the C.N.I.T., on the axis of the voie triomphale (p. 14). The E.P.A.D. excluded zone A, and after an interval on R.A.T.P. ground two kilometres to the west, the project settled about one kilometre west of the C.N.I.T., facing the museum Le Corbusier was to build, on ground at 52 m, against the S.N.C.F. land at La Folie, between the A14 and the Saint-Germain branch of the R.E.R. (pp. 14–15, 70; plans pp. 133, 135). The plot on p. 135 is fixed by three features: the edge of the S.N.C.F. yards at La Folie to the north, the C.D. 131 at its western end, and the R.E.R. cutting to the south. Laid against the I.G.N. aerial survey of 1965–1980, where the yards and the R.E.R. works are visible, these place the socle at roughly 48.8974° N, 2.2229° E, about 130 m east of the point where the C.D. 131 meets the railway land, with zone A a kilometre to the east as p. 70 has it. The result depends on reading the C.D. 131 correctly in the photograph and should be taken as good to a hundred and fifty metres or so; the plan carries no scale or coordinates, and its “station M” on the Saint-Germain branch does not correspond to any station built. The map defaults to the I.G.N. aerial survey of 1965–1980, which shows the ground as it stood when the site was chosen.

Light, beams and reflections

The published images, the night renderings of pp. 87 and 89 and the covers of Paris Match, make the tower a fountain of searchlight beams. The inventory points elsewhere. The 2,250 spotlights are of medium power and carry coloured devices (p. 26), and Annex 2 places the projectors and flashes in 343 cabinets set in the windows of the structure, “destinées à éclairer les miroirs” (p. 122): the greater part of the light was to be thrown inward, onto the 263 rotating mirrors, and returned by them as moving coloured reflections, in the manner of Schöffer’s luminodynamic work. The 2,085 flashes burst in every direction. Only sixty-four sources form beams, the forty marine projectors with a throw of two kilometres, some set at the summit “pour prolonger la hauteur de la Tour la nuit” (p. 27), and the twenty-four lasers whose rays the mirrors were to multiply; all sixty-four were disconnected by day (p. 100), and their beams would have been visible along their length only in haze. The rendering here shares the renderings’ bias: projectors are drawn as points of light, not aimed at the mirrors, and the bloom lends the forty beams more weight than the inventory gives them.

The mirrors and the wind

The 114 axes are “mus par des moteurs électriques”, at variable speed in slow, medium and fast groups (p. 26); nothing in the text has the wind turn them, and the wind reaches the mirrors only by way of the anemometers and the programme. The rendering keeps to this. An option in Réglages lets idle mirrors swing in the gusts, as a 150 m² panel on an unbraked axis would; it is outside the text and off by default.

Inputs

The ten sensors that set the régime are those of the 1973 simulation: four microphones of increasing sensitivity, four anemometers at different altitudes, and two road-traffic sensors that fire when a Poisson draw exceeds its mean (p. 115). The anemometers read the winds given by Open-Meteo at 10, 80, 120 and 180 m above La Défense, which answers fairly closely to Schöffer’s “capteurs de vent supposés à des altitudes différentes”; the road sensors take their mean from the Ville de Paris counters. Without a microphone on site, sound is estimated from the same counts by the CoRTN relation, unless the viewer’s own microphone is switched on. All twenty-eight channels enter the functions Xi as xj, and the remote ones, averaged, give the “tonalité générale” (p. 113) by lowering or raising the thresholds Ki.

The teletype has been replaced by whatever a browser may still request without credentials, and the substitutions should be read as substitutions. The P.T.T. become two rates of transmission: French-language posts on Bluesky for the telephones, edits to the French Wikipedia for the telegraphs. The noise at the back of the Chamber becomes a compound of sitting hours, the LCP wire and public posts naming the Assembly. Air navigation is reduced to aeronautical meteorology at Roissy, since no source of air traffic will answer a browser. The Préfecture’s counts are published a day late, so, taking Schöffer at his word that the city unfolds “un programme type de rythmes qui se déroulent sur 24 heures” (p. 29), the prototype reads the previous day at the present hour. The Bourse and the R.A.T.P./S.N.C.F. lines need free keys (Réglages). The sapeurs-pompiers have no open real-time source and remain simulated, by the Poisson method of 1973. Frame stresses are computed from the live wind with Schöffer’s own structural figures (pp. 126–127: Sv = 5,850 m², global coefficient 1.5, design shear 840 t) and from EMSC seismicity.

Degrees of fidelity

Three registers should be kept apart. Taken from the text: the functions Xi = 1 when Σ Aijxj − K > 0, with Aij random or null; the hundreds split by lot into five classes and the head number that assigns them; θi = Yi/Ni; D over ten binary sensors and its three régimes; the validating draw against 0.60, 0.75 and 0.90; the Poisson rule for the road sensors; the table of the shutter projectors; the night schedule and the daytime disconnection of the large projectors and lasers; the eight types of programme-parameter. Readings of ambiguous passages: the joining of the two programmes, which folds the simulation’s own output functions Fi = Σ CijXej − Bi into the general Xi; the tonality acting on K; the organigramme’s “Bi = max” taken as a gate on colour; the 60, 75 and 90 per cent taken as brightness, where they might equally be the share of organs lit (the Comparer tab runs both). Decisions where the text defers to “critères artistiques”, listed below.

Where the text is silent

Each Xi is sensitive to three to seven inputs, with Aij uniform on (0, 1). Ki is set against the running mean of its inputs, so that a function fires a little more than half the time. Seventy-two per cent of the groups carry a function and the rest are at rest. Yi follows a gamma law whose mean depends on the type of organ. Functions are reassigned every five minutes, the period Stener Jørgensen and Laplante-Anfossi suggest. The fifty modes (“50 environ”, p. 111) are the product of ten orderings and five envelopes, both taken from the vocabulary of p. 101. The cellule d’indifférence watches for a recurring peak in the autocorrelation of activity, for saturation and stagnation relative to the evening’s own mean, and for a régime held longer than ten minutes; it answers by redrawing a third of the sensitivities and by antagonist flashes (pp. 29, 100, 115). The disappearance in smoke lasts ten minutes, after Stener Jørgensen (2024), and is set off by a felt earthquake, by the city’s threshold of excitation, or by hand. Gusts are interpolated between hourly winds; the noise estimate and the seismic attenuation are orders of magnitude only.

Textual difficulties

The printed formula reads θi = Ni/Yi, while the sentence introducing it divides Yi by Ni; the prototype follows the sentence. The régimes are given as D ≤ 3, 3 < D < 8 and D ≥ 8 in Annex 1, and as “3 ou moins”, “entre 4 et 7” and “entre 8 et 10” on p. 114, which agree for integers. The inventory of p. 26 (4,664 parameters of action) differs from that of Annex 2 (2,592 Cibié projectors, 1,977 Orthotron flashes, 60 Fulgor, 12 arc projectors, 343 cabinets); the former has been used. The night renderings paint the frame pale, where p. 26 specifies Corten steel; the rendering here sits between the two.

Instruments

Stener Jørgensen and Laplante-Anfossi argue that a programme which treats every input as so much volume of information, passed through several probability laws, would be unpredictable locally and self-similar globally, a “big blur” in which the tower behaves as though it were no longer interacting with its environment. Three instruments bear on the claim. The Analyse tab records every step and estimates how much of the tower’s variation the twenty-eight inputs explain (R²), which inputs it follows, and how quickly its fluctuations average out as the window widens. The Comparer tab runs a second programme on the same data under different criteria, so that what belongs to Paris can be separated from what belongs to the laws of chance. The Archives tab replays a past day, so that an ordinary Tuesday can be set against a day of strikes or storms.

Tour Lumière Cybernétique

A working reconstruction of Nicolas Schöffer’s programme for the unbuilt tower at La Défense (1973). It takes its immediate and remote inputs from present-day Paris and runs them through the computer programme, simulation régimes and cellule d’indifférence described in the book’s appendices.

Starting it connects to about a dozen public data services and, if checked, analyses two Radio France live streams without playing them aloud.
